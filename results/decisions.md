# Decision log

Dated, with reasoning. This becomes the methods section.

## 2026-09-27 — scope
Vertical slice first: human reviewed UniProt entries with CARBOHYD features.
Rationale: smallest dataset that can produce a real PR-AUC this week.

## 2026-09-27 — negative-set policy
Unannotated candidate residues labelled 0 but treated as UNLABELED in
interpretation. Naive labelling used only to establish a baseline number.
Positive-unlabeled learning is the intended methodological contribution.

## 2026-09-27 — splits
Homology-aware clustering (MMseqs2, 30% identity) required for every reported
metric. Random splits leak family identity. Fallback per-accession split exists
so the pipeline runs end-to-end but is explicitly marked non-reportable.

## 2026-09-27 — metric
PR-AUC as primary. ROC-AUC reported alongside but not headline: prevalence is
low single-digit percent, where ROC-AUC flatters everything.

## 2026-09-27 — evidence-code filtering added
Measured: PR-AUC 0.813 on all UniProt N-site annotations vs 0.474 on
experimental-only (ECO:0000269 / ECO:0007744). 13,490 of 16,072 positives are
ECO:0000255, generated by sequon matching. `--experimental-only` is now required
for any reportable N-site number. Positives lacking experimental evidence are
DROPPED, not demoted to negative -- absence of experimental evidence is not
evidence of absence.

## 2026-09-27 — one-hot logreg == PSSM
Both scored 0.474 to three decimals on experimental N-sites. A linear model over
one-hot positions is effectively a PSSM. Consequence: the baseline to beat is the
PSSM, not the sequon. Any claim of improvement must clear 0.474.

## 2026-09-27 — literature check changed the framing
Checked ESM-LoRA-Gly (2025) and StackGlyEmbed (2025) methods. Both use CD-HIT at
30% identity and glycoproteomics-derived positives, so the ECO:0000255 critique
does NOT apply to them. But both draw negatives from separate non-glycoproteins,
sequon-filter them, and balance classes ~1:1. The 2019 PU-learning paper names
balanced test sets as its own limitation. The gap nobody has closed: evaluation
at natural prevalence across all candidate residues in glycoproteins. That is
what this pipeline does, and it is why these numbers are ~0.47 rather than ~0.98.

## 2026-09-27 — scope reduced to exploratory
User is drafting ideas, not targeting publication. Dropped the protocol-comparison
harness. Added subsample.py and cleanup.py; switched one-hot to uint8 and
memory-mapped feature loading to keep the footprint small.

## 2026-09-27 — ESM mean-pooling is wrong for this task
ESM-2 35M + MLP scored 0.138, well below one-hot's 0.474. Cause: featurize.py
mean-pools the 21-mer window, producing a vector with no positional information
about the candidate residue. `--pooling centre` added as the correct alternative.
Not yet run. The 0.138 row must not be cited as an ESM result.

## 2026-09-27 — centre-residue pooling confirms the featurizer bug
ESM-2 35M + MLP: 0.138 with mean-pooling, 0.497 with centre-residue pooling.
The bug was mine, not the model's. But 0.497 vs PSSM 0.475 is a tie, not an
improvement: ESM loses on fold 1, and the per-fold difference ranges -0.048 to
+0.056 against a mean of +0.022. On a single seed with ~620 positives per fold
this is indistinguishable from noise.

Interpretation: a 21-residue window gives ESM no more information than a PSSM
has, so it cannot use the long-range context that justifies a language model.
Next tests, in order of expected value: widen WINDOW in parse_sites.py to 25 or
50 (51- or 101-mer), then try esm2_t30_150M_UR50D. Neither run yet.
